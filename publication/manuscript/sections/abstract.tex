Segmentation of atomic resolution images in transmission electron microscopy is a critical step for quantifying crystal structure and defects in materials. Deep learning models are regularly used to localize atomic columns in periodic lattices and known defect structures, but struggle to generalize to unknown structures. Here, we introduce Blob-Net, a deep learning model trained on simulated images of randomly placed atoms instead of atoms in a lattice. This training eliminates encoding crystal geometry priors into the network, resulting in a network that remains competitive on periodic lattices and improves model predictions on nonperiodic lattices and defect structures such as boundaries and edges. This new approach in atomic segmentation enables more robust analysis of unidentified atomic structures where interesting scientific phenomena may be present, and which are difficult to analyze reliably by other methods.
